How stock works,
for each kind of business

A restaurant counts rice in grams and sells plates. A shop counts packets and sells packets. A salon sells a relaxer and uses 50 ml of it. A mall doesn't own the goods at all. This guide explains how BizNez handles each of them, what to show a business when you sell it, how to set it up on the first day, and how to answer the questions they will ask you.

How to use this guide
  1. Read Part 1 once: the basics every business gets, whatever it sells.
  2. Before a sales visit, read the page for that kind of business in Part 2.
  3. When a business calls with a problem, go to Part 4: Support. Find what they see on the screen, then what to tell them.

Button names in this guide are written like this, exactly as they appear on the screen, so you can tell a business which button to press.

The one-sentence pitch

"Every sale takes stock off by itself, you see what's running low before it runs out, and once a month you count everything and BizNez tells you, in shillings, what has gone missing."

The three numbers every owner cares about
  • What I have: Stock → What you have, per shop and store, with what it's worth at buying price.
  • What's running low: orange in the same list, and Only show items running low.
  • What's gone missing: the result of a stock-take (Part 3), in money, and kept for every month.
Part 1

The basics every business gets

These are on every plan and in every kind of business. Stock counting is never optional in BizNez: it comes with selling.

How stock goes up and down

Nobody types stock changes by hand day to day. Every one of these moves stock by itself and is written in Stock history with who did it and when:

Setting up stock on the first day

  1. Add the products. Items → Add product, or Add many at once (from Excel) for a long list. For each product tick Count how many I have.
  2. Say how many they have now. The new-product form asks How many do you have now? In Excel it is the How many you have column. That becomes the starting stock.
  3. Set the warning level. Warn me when only this many are left: when stock reaches it, the item turns orange.
  4. Photos and barcodes (optional, but sell them). A photo shows on the till buttons; Add photos to many items takes a batch of photos and matches them by file name (call the photo Sugar 2kg.jpg). Barcodes let the till scan.
Excel import, with or without photos. The import window asks first: Without photos (add them later, or never) or With a photo link for each item. The columns are Name, Category, Barcode, Code, Sold by, Selling price, Buying price, How many you have, Warn when this low, and Photo link. Download an empty template gives the right headings.

Shops, stores and branches

Stock is kept per place. A business starts with its main shop and a store inside it. A shop with a separate store or warehouse adds it under Stock → Add shop or store, then uses Move stock when goods go from the store to the shop. Staff who work at a branch see that branch's stock and sell from it.

Part 2

Shops and minimarts

also: hardware, cosmetics shops, electronics, boutiques, agro-vets

How their stock really works

They buy in cartons and sell in pieces, mostly the same day. Fast items (sugar, bread, milk, airtime) run out without warning; slow items sit for months. Losses come from theft, breakage, items given on credit and not recorded, and perishables that go off.

What BizNez does for them

  • Barcode scanning at the till; each sale takes the piece off stock.
  • Orange warning when an item reaches its level, and Only show items running low to make the shopping list.
  • Store feeds shop: extra stock in the back store, Move stock to the shop when the shelf is low.
  • Buying from suppliers (Growth): restock suggestions from what's low, an order to the supplier, then receive when the lorry comes: stock goes up, the buying price updates, and what they owe that supplier goes up.
  • Expiry on perishables: when receiving, type the expiry date; the oldest is sold first and shows in Use soon.
  • The till refuses to sell what isn't there ("Not enough Sugar 2kg: only 0 left in Main shop") so the count stays honest. Settings can allow selling at zero for shops that receive goods without recording them (not recommended).

First day

Excel list with How many you have → photos → warning levels on the fast items → suppliers (Growth) → a first stock-take within the first week to start clean.

Routine to teach them

Daily: check orange items. When goods arrive: receive the order (or Fix count if they don't use suppliers). Monthly: a stock-take, or one category a day for a big shop.

What sells it: "Last month you lost KES 4,000 of stock and didn't know which items. With BizNez, the stock-take tells you: 2 sugar, 1 cooking oil, worth so much." Show them a finished stock-take.

Wholesalers and distributors

also: cash & carry, building materials, drinks distributors

How their stock really works

Large quantities, prices that drop for bigger orders, goods kept in a warehouse and sent to branches or customers, buyers who ask for a quotation first, and a lot of credit both ways.

What BizNez does for them

  • Wholesale price per product with the quantity it starts from: the till charges it by itself above that quantity.
  • Quotes (Growth) that turn into the sale with one tap; stock only goes down on the sale.
  • Warehouse → branches with Move stock; each branch's stock separately.
  • Suppliers, deliveries and balances; customers on credit and who owes what.
  • A Store keeper job: receives and moves stock without seeing the till or the money.
What sells it: the store keeper can't see the money and the cashier can't change stock; the owner sees both. Every move between the warehouse and a branch has a name and a time.

Restaurants, cafés, bars, bakeries and grills

also: fast food, canteens, cloud kitchens, ice cream

How their stock really works

This is the most different. They buy ingredients (a 2 kg bag of rice, a crate of soda, a bottle of whisky) and sell plates and tots. Nobody counts plates in stock: they count rice in grams and whisky in millilitres. Losses come from waste, over-portioning, staff meals and bottles that "evaporate" at the bar.

What BizNez does for them

  • Ingredients (Recipes → Add an ingredient): kept in a cooking unit (g, ml, pc) and bought in a buying unit with its size (a bag of 2000 g at KES 260). BizNez works out the cost per gram.
  • What one plate uses (Set what one plate uses): chicken biryani = 200 g rice + 250 g chicken + … Each plate sold takes those off the kitchen stock, and the plate's cost and food cost % show.
  • Choices that change the recipe: "with chapati instead of rice" swaps the ingredient.
  • Batch recipes: a pot of stew or a tray of chapati dough is made once (made in the kitchen) and then used by many dishes.
  • Dishes go "finished" by themselves when an ingredient they always use runs out, and come back when a delivery is recorded. Waiters stop selling what the kitchen can't make.
  • Bar: a bottle is 750 ml; Set tot and bottle prices sells by the tot (25 ml), the glass or the bottle. A shortage on a count is shown in tots and in lost sales, not just millilitres.
  • Waste: the kitchen records burnt, spilled or gone-off food with a reason; the owner can choose that only managers record waste.
  • Kitchen count: count the ingredients, see what's missing at cost (and in lost sales for the bar).
  • Use-by dates: when recording a delivery (Record a delivery), the optional Use by date keeps that delivery as its own batch, used first, shown in Use soon.
  • Bakeries: the daily baking list, and a day-old price for yesterday's bread. Grills: meat sold by the kg, weighed at the till.

First day

Menu (dishes) → ingredients with buying units and today's count → what each plate uses, starting with the 10 best sellers → bar bottles with tot prices. Don't try to put recipes on every dish on day one: start with the expensive ingredients (meat, chicken, fish, spirits).

Routine to teach them

Every delivery: Record a delivery (with use-by for milk, meat, vegetables). Every night: record waste. Weekly: a kitchen count of meat and spirits. Monthly: the full stock-take.

What sells it: "Your chicken should give you 40 plates a week. If the count says you used chicken for 48, someone is giving extra or taking it home." Show the food cost % on their best seller and the bar count in lost sales.
Be clear: restaurants may sell below zero (the kitchen often has more than the records say). Dishes only go "finished" for ingredients they have actually counted or received, so a new restaurant doesn't see its whole menu disappear on day one.

Hotels, lodges and guest houses

with a restaurant and bar, or rooms only

How their stock really works

Three kinds of stock: the restaurant and bar (as above), the housekeeping supplies that are used, not sold (soap, toilet paper, cleaning), and linen and towels that go round and round and slowly go missing or get damaged. Some have a minibar.

What BizNez does for them

  • Restaurant and bar: everything in the restaurant page, and bills can be put on the guest's room.
  • Housekeeping supplies: Record stock used in-house with a purpose (Housekeeping, Room 12, Laundry). Front desk and housekeeping staff can record it. Stock → Used in-house adds up the month by purpose, in money.
  • Linen and towels: keep them as products with a count; record damaged or lost ones as used in-house (Towels and linen damaged); count them on the monthly stock-take.
  • Minibar: minibar items are products; a minibar sale is sold at the till and put on the room's bill.
What sells it: "How much soap and toilet paper does one room-night cost you?" The used-in-house report answers it.
Not built yet: a linen cycle (clean → in the room → laundry → back) and a minibar check per room at check-out. Say so; they are on the list.

Salons, barbers, spas, nail bars and car washes

also: gyms with a shop, beauty clinics

How their stock really works

Two kinds of stock: products they sell to clients (hair oil, lotion) and products they use up doing the service (relaxer, gel polish, shampoo, car-wash soap). The second kind is where the money leaks: a tub that should do 20 clients does 12.

What BizNez does for them

  • Products for sale: counted like a shop, sold at the till with the service on the same bill.
  • Products a service uses (open the service → Products this service uses → Add a product it uses): e.g. 50 ml relaxer per "Relaxer & set". Each service sold takes that off the relaxer's stock.
  • A service is never hidden because a product shows zero (the records may be behind); the stock-take shows the truth.
  • Stylists can record products used outside a service (Used on a client).
  • Commissions per stylist (Growth).
What sells it: "This tub is 1,000 ml; at 50 ml a client it should do 20 relaxers. The stock-take will tell you if it did 12."

Services and tour operators

also: schools, garages, printers, tailors, laundries

Most of them sell time, not goods, so stock is optional. Where they do keep stock (a garage's spare parts, a printer's paper and ink, a laundry's soap), they use it like a shop, and supplies used on a job are recorded as used in-house with the job as the purpose. Tour operators sell seats on trips, not stock.

Malls, markets and landlords

also: food courts, plazas, rental flats

How it is different

The mall or market does not own the goods. Its "stock" is units and stalls. Each stall's own goods belong to that stall.

  • The landlord side: units and stalls, leases, rent, meters, daily market fees, the food court.
  • Food court: each food stall's dishes are on one food court menu that customers order from.
  • The mall's own supplies (cleaning, toilet paper for common areas) can be recorded as used in-house.
  • A stall that wants stock control opens its own BizNez shop. That is the upsell in every mall: one landlord, many tenant shops.
Not built yet: a stall's own BizNez shop linked to the mall (the mall seeing its tenants' sales or turnover). It is on the later list.
Part 3

The monthly stock-take, step by step

This is the feature that pays for BizNez. Teach every business to do it in the first week (to start clean) and then once a month.

  1. Stock → Stock-take → Start a stock-take. Only the owner, a manager, a store keeper or the accountant can start one.
  2. Choose the place (if they have more than one) and Everything or one category. Big shop: one category a day.
  3. Leave Don't show counters what the records say ticked. Counters then count what's really there instead of copying the number.
  4. Counters open Count and finish on their phones. Several people can count at once. They type the number for each item; it saves when they leave the box. Search or scan finds an item; Only what's not counted yet shows what's left.
  5. The owner watches the shortage so far, item by item: "missing 2 (KES 120)".
  6. When counting is done: Finish the stock-take → Yes, put the counts into my stock.
What happens when they finish
  • Every counted item becomes the stock.
  • Items nobody counted stay as they were.
  • Missing and extra are kept, in money at buying price, with the stock-take's number.
  • A shortage of KES 500 or more shows as a warning in the activity log.
Advice to give them
  • Count when the shop is closed or quiet: sales during the count blur the result.
  • Record all deliveries before counting.
  • Extra stock usually means a delivery that wasn't recorded.
  • The same item short every month? Look at who sold it and when (Pro: full history).

Restaurants also have the quick kitchen count on the Recipes page for ingredients only; the stock-take covers everything.
