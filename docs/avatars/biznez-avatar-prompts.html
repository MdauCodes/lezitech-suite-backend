BizNez avatar prompts

For generating the people shown on the BizNez home page, features pages, demo picker, empty screens and tips: one after the other, with any image AI. Same family look as the Trendysh avatars (photo-real, waist up, plain white background, holding a tool of their trade), but dressed the way Kenyan SME people really dress at work: smart casual, aprons, overalls, kitenge, polo shirts. No suits.

1. How to use this

  1. Paste the master style first, then one person prompt below it, as one prompt.
  2. Add the avoid list as the negative prompt (or as "Avoid: …" at the end if the tool has no negative box).
  3. Make 2 to 4 tries and keep the most natural one: relaxed smile, real hands, the object clearly visible.
  4. Save it with the file name given, at least 1200 × 1500 px, then let LeziTech cut out the background and make the small sizes.
  5. Keep faces different from each other: never reuse a face for two people (Trendysh learned this with Nails and Brows).

2. Master style (paste first, every time)

Photorealistic portrait photograph of a Kenyan small-business person at work, waist-up, facing the camera with a slight turn, warm genuine smile, natural relaxed pose, holding a tool of their trade clearly in their hands. Dressed in smart-casual or work clothes typical of Nairobi small businesses (not a suit). Real skin texture, natural hair, modest jewellery. Plain pure white background, soft even studio light from the front-left, gentle shadow, sharp focus on the face and hands, 85 mm lens, f/4. Clean edges suitable for cutting out. Portrait orientation 4:5, the person centred with space above the head.

3. Avoid list (negative prompt)

suit, tie, corporate office, stock-photo pose, exaggerated grin, plastic skin, airbrushed, heavy makeup on men, extra fingers, deformed hands, text, logos, brand names, watermark, busy background, props floating, cartoon, illustration, 3D render, sunglasses, face cut off, hands cut off, Western-looking setting, snow.

4. The people

Mix of ages (20s to 60s), men and women, and communities across Kenya. Names in brackets are only to keep track; don't put names in the prompt.
